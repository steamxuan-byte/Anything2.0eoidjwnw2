Does online multiplayer gaming build real friendships, or leave students more alone?

The big question behind this exhibition

Perspectives Zone

Three groups see the same game night very differently. Here is what each one notices, and why.

Students

Gaming is a main social hub for teamwork, communication and unwinding after school.

Why they see it this way

Multiplayer games give teens a shared goal, instant voice chat with friends, and a low-pressure space to hang out when they can’t meet in person.

Parents

Gaming often disrupts healthy sleep routines and reduces quality time with family at home.

Why they see it this way

Parents see the immediate offline effects: late-night screen use, irritability from lack of sleep, and teens spending hours alone in their bedrooms.

Teachers

Heavy gaming can lower classroom focus, affect academic performance and weaken face-to-face communication skills.

Why they see it this way

Teachers observe students’ daytime alertness, homework completion, and how comfortably they collaborate in person during lessons.

Our verdict

Based on our interviews and secondary research, online multiplayer gaming both connects and isolates students, depending on how it is balanced. Most students use gaming to strengthen existing friendships and build teamwork, but excessive late-night play contributes to sleep loss and lower classroom focus. Ultimately, gaming fosters real community when it complements offline friendships rather than replacing face-to-face interaction and rest.

Beyond the Headset: Community or Isolation?

Research question

To what extent does online multiplayer gaming foster genuine social connections versus increase real-world isolation among students?

Description

Online multiplayer games have become the modern after-school hangout, yet students and adults often disagree on their impact. Through interviews with students, parents, and teachers—alongside psychological and educational research—this exhibition explores whether gaming builds genuine friendships or creates offline distance. Visitors can compare three competing viewpoints, test their assumptions with our interactive flip-cards, and add their own voice to our live community vote.
